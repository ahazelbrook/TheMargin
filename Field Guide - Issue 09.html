This week's letter · Midwinter

The shortest day of the year, spent the long way round.

This week: a glass-cabin gondola into the cold bright air above the bay, a kitchen-garden lunch on the Sorrento strip, and a six-metre fire on the foreshore as the longest night comes down.

Some weekends are for staying close to home. This is not one of them. The solstice falls on Sunday, which makes Saturday the year's last full slide into the dark — and one place down south does something proper with it. So we're giving the inner west the morning off and pointing the car toward the Peninsula for a single unhurried day that ends in fire. It's a bigger day than we usually ask of you, so we've kept it to three things and built in the gaps: up the mountain while everyone's fresh, a long lunch in a garden, then the foreshore at dusk. Warm layers, a thermos, no particular hurry.

i. The Morning Shift

Start the shortest day as high as you can get.

A glass cabin, fifteen slow minutes over the treetops, and the whole bay laid out grey and enormous below.

The Eagle is a gondola — a proper one, glass-cabined and unhurried — that lifts you from the foot of Arthurs Seat to its 314-metre summit in about fifteen minutes, the bushland dropping away and Port Phillip widening the higher you go. On a clear winter morning you can see the city one way and Geelong the other, and on most mornings there are kangaroos grazing in the scrub directly beneath the cabin, which is the kind of thing a four-year-old does not forget.

At the top there's the Eagle Café for a coffee you've earned by doing nothing, viewing decks, and the Seawinds Gardens — formal lawns, a scatter of William Ricketts sculptures, and enough room to let everyone stretch before the day proper begins. Go early: the gondola gets busier as the morning warms, and you want the mountain while the light is still doing its best work.¹

While you're there
  • a.Seawinds GardensArthurs Seat summit — free formal gardens and sculpture walks; kangaroos in the morning.
  • b.Enchanted Adventure GardenA few minutes from the base — hedge mazes, tube slides and tree-surfing, if the kids have energy to burn.
  • c.Dromana foreshoreAt the foot of the mountain — a flat, calm bay beach for a quick leg-stretch on the way through.
The logistics
  • WhereBase Station, Arthurs Seat Road, Dromana — a little over an hour from the inner west
  • WhenDaily from mid-morning; the ride up and back is about thirty minutes, plus time at the top
  • Best atFirst thing, while the gondola's quiet and the light is low
  • PramA folded pram travels in the cabin; the summit paths and gardens are mostly pram-friendly

¹The gondola opened in 2016, replacing a chairlift that had carried people up the mountain since the 1960s.

ii. The Afternoon Reset

Lunch in a garden, on the way to the fire.

A former nursery turned kitchen-garden café on the Sorrento strip, with herb boxes for walls and a burger worth the drive on its own.

The Sisters sits at the quiet end of Ocean Beach Road, a kitchen-garden café built in an old nursery, its courtyard still hemmed with garden boxes of herbs and winter veg. It is unfussy and genuinely good with children — shakes, a cabinet of home-baked cakes, room to fidget — and a proper sit-down treat for you before an afternoon on your feet. The Sisters Burger is the order. Get there on the early side of lunch, so you're fed and watered well before the foreshore fills.

The order
  • For youThe Sisters Burger — beef, cheddar, garden-herb aioli, fries
  • For themA thickshake and something from the cabinet
  • WhenEarly lunch, before the festival crowd
  • SideThe lemon-zucchini muffin, if it's on

The courtyard's the move on a still day, the warm room on a cold one.

iii. · This week's discovery

And then, on the longest night, they set the bay alight.

A free foreshore festival for the winter solstice — lanterns, two stages, and a six-metre effigy that burns into the dark over the water.

This is the reason for the drive. As dusk comes down on the shortest day, Sorrento's foreshore fills with lantern light, two stages of local music, food trucks and mulled wine, a silent disco, and — at half past six — Bernie: a six-metre effigy, said to be the largest in Victoria, set alight against the black water by a pyrotechnics crew while the whole town stands at the edge of the bay and watches. It is genuinely affecting, and it is free.

For the children, the afternoon is the thing — a Kids Zone of lantern-making, face painting and giant bubbles, and the lantern-lit main street as the light goes. For you, once the small ones are wrangled, there's a pop-up sauna on the foreshore from Peninsula Hot Springs, mulled wine in the cold, and a silent disco if the mood takes you — the closest thing to a night out that still counts as a family day.

The honest part: the burn is at half past six, which is cold, dark and well past small dinners. The family-friendly window is really the afternoon into dusk; whether you stay for Bernie is a judgement call on your particular kids, and there's no shame in watching the first flames and then driving home to a warm bath. Parking is tight — there's a free shuttle from four o'clock between the Sorrento Surf Club and the Melbourne Road roundabout, so park once and ride in.

A note in the margin

We started Field Guide for the weekends that hold both of you at once — the children and the people you were before them. There's something right about marking the year's lowest point not by hiding from it but by driving an hour and a half to stand in the cold and watch a fire, together. The light comes back from here.

— Field Guide
